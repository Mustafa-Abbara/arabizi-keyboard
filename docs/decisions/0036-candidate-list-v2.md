# ADR 0036: A wider, learned ↑ list (word suggestions v2)

- **Status:** Accepted, 10 Nov 2026
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Deciders:** Moustafa Abbara
- **Stage:** P7 (reliability), core, engine and both keyboards

## Context

When the model writes a word wrong, ↑ (or ⌥Tab / Alt+Tab) offers other spellings (ADR 0027). The user asked that typing a word letter by letter ("Spell it") become a last resort. Measured on 10 Nov 2026 over every wrong word v3 made on four sets (1,298 words: the private WhatsApp and senzi sheets, dev and dev-others), the right word is in today's list (7 places, the last keeping the typed word) for **51%** of wrong words, first for 30%.

Why it misses:
- **Half of the right words are not among the ~13,000 Arabic words Harfi knows,** and today's list only offers known words, one letter from the model's word or from a dictionary neighbour.
- **Typed words are spelled more ways than one edit covers:** `t` is ت or ط, a middle `a` is usually not written (`kifak` → كيفك), a final `e` is ي or ة, a prefix may be typed joined (`wamen` → وآمن).

Experiments (scratchpad, aggregate numbers only) compared three sources and their order:
- **Re-scoring the model's own sentence** changed almost nothing (+0.1 points). The model already used the sentence when it chose. Dropped.
- **Spelling the typed word every plausible way, keeping only known words:** +3 to +9 points. Capped by the vocabulary.
- **The same, keeping unknown words, ranked by how typists write each letter** (odds learned from the training pairs): large gains on WhatsApp and dev, losses on senzi when unknown words fill the list. A mix is needed.
- **The model's own top guesses for that spot:** the right word is among its 10 guesses for 73% of wrong words, including words no dictionary knows.

## Decision

| # | Decision | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seven candidate sources** for the selected word: (1) the model's top guesses for that spot in its answer; (2) spellings of the typed word ranked by letter odds, **unknown words kept**; (3) known spellings of the typed word (the same odds, known words only); (4) the training data's own spellings of the typed word; (5) a prefix typed joined (`w`, `b`, `l`, `el`, `bel`…) split off, the rest spelled, the Arabic prefix put back; (6) long-vowel, emphatic and ending variants of the model's word, unknown kept; (7) today's list (ADR 0027). | User (the goal); sources **Recommendation**, measured |
| 2 | **Letter odds** (`data/rules/spelling_odds.json`, generated by `pairs spelling-odds` from training rows only): for each Arabizi unit (`t`, `sh`, `aa`, `2`…) at the start, middle or end of a word, how often typists wrote each Arabic letter, or nothing. A missing long vowel after a consonant followed by a consonant (`kfk` → كيفك) is one of the options, with its own odds. | **Recommendation** |
| 3 | **Order: a small learned ranker** (logistic regression on about 30 signals per candidate: the model's confidence and rank, typing likelihood under the letter odds, known word, frequency, rank in today's list, how many sources agree, closeness to the model's word and to the rules' spelling, a letter-pattern score…), weights in `data/rules/candidate_ranker.json`. **Trained on public rows only** (dev and dev-others, 439 wrong words); WhatsApp and senzi stay unseen tests. Trees did no better (±1 point). The user's own earlier fixes still come first (ADR 0027 #8). | **Recommendation**, measured |
| 4 | **Instant first, model after.** The list appears at once from sources 2–7 and the ranker (milliseconds); the model's guesses (source 1) arrive in the background and are merged in. The highlighted word never moves; model guesses only take places below it. A slow, busy or missing model never delays the list. | User (smooth feel); **Recommendation** |
| 5 | **The model's guesses:** a new engine request (API contract v2): the typed text, the model's answer up to the word, and how many guesses. A small beam (5 guesses, all branches decoded together, prefix computed once). It starts right after a conversion for doubtful (dashed) words, and on ↑ for others. Target: under 400 ms on the 8 GB Air and the Windows laptop, measured before it is turned on. | **Recommendation** |
| 6 | **Parity:** the Python reference (`eval`) and Swift (`ArabiziCore`) build the same candidates and signals; fixtures generated by Python are checked by Swift tests, as for the rules (B0). | **Recommendation** |
| 7 | **Privacy:** no new data is stored. The model request carries the message being fixed, in memory only, never logged (as conversions). | **Recommendation** (ADR 0018) |

## Consequences

Measured on 10 Nov 2026, each set scored while left out of training. "Top 7" means 6 Arabic places, the last keeping the typed word.

| Right word… | Today | Instant list (no model) | + model's top 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| first, WhatsApp / senzi | 34% / 32% | 54% / 49% | 56% / 59% |
| in the top 3 | 47% / 45% | 69% / 64% | 74% / 75% |
| in the top 7 | 48% / 59% | 78% / 78% | 82% / 81% |

- On all four sets (the ranker trained on the other three each time): first 30% → 55%, top 3 43% → 72%, top 7 51% → 80%, two pages of 7 56% → 85%. The right word is in some source for 90%; the other 10% (new words far from what was typed, some merged words) still need "Spell it".
- Real use should do better: the user's own fixes go first, which the test sets cannot show.
- **Cost:** two generated files (letter odds, ranker weights); a model request per doubtful word, in the background. The latency target (#5) is measured before the model's guesses are turned on; until then the instant list ships alone.
- **Limits:** words not written one for one (merged or split by the model) are offered only through the model's guesses; a bigger vocabulary and a v4 model are what would move the remaining 10%.
